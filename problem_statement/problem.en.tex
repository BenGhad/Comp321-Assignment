\begin{center}
\textbf{\Large I Can't See the Screen!}
\end{center}

A cinema has $R$ rows and $C$ columns of seats, with row $0$ being furthest from the screen, and row $R-1$ the closest. The floor is sloped downward towards the screen such that each row is exactly $1ft$ higher than the row in front of it, with the first row having a height of $0ft$.

The seats in the cinema are represented by an $R \times C$ matrix $H$, where $H[r][c]$ represents the height in $ft$ of the person sitting in row $r$, column $c$.

\begin{center}

\illustration{0.35}{cinema.png}{Photo by Felix Mooneeram on Unsplash}
\end{center}

\subsection*{Visibility Rule}

A person sitting in position $(r, c)$ cannot see the screen if there exists someone sitting in position $(k, c)$ ($k > r$) who is at least as tall in absolute position, accounting for both their physical height and the elevation of their row.

Among all such people sitting in row $k$ who block the view, only the nearest one (the smallest $k > r$) matters.

If no such person exists, the person sitting in position $(r, c)$ can see the screen.

\subsection*{Input}
Input starts with a line containing two space-separated integers $m$ and $n$ ($1 \leq m, n \leq 10000$) that represent the dimensions of $H$. Each of the next $m$ lines contain $n$ space-separated integers that represent the height in $ft$ of the person in the seat. For each $H[r][c]$, if the seat is empty, $H[r][c] = -1$. Otherwise, $1 \leq H[r][c] \leq 2^{32} - 1$.

\subsection*{Output}

Print an $R \times C$ grid of integers. For each $H[r][c]$, output $-1$ if the seat is empty or if the person can see the screen. Otherwise, output the row index $k$ of the nearest person in front of them who blocks their view.
